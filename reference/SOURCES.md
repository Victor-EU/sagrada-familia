# Reference photographs — where they came from and what each one is for

Twenty-eight frames, 49.3 MB, inside and out. Every one is from Wikimedia Commons
under a licence that permits reuse, and every one is recorded below with its author,
its licence and a link back to the file page. They are harness input: nothing here is
bundled into the build.

## How they were sized

Commons serves a fixed ladder of widths, so each file was taken at the widest step the
budget allowed rather than at its original size. Frames whose **detail** is the thing
being matched — the canopy, the vault surface, the column flutes, the two glazings, the
current silhouette — are at **3840 px**. Frames that carry **proportion and framing**
rather than texture are at **1920 px**, which is already more than the overlay can show
on any display the app runs on. Gaudí's plan is the one file taken at its original size:
its resolution *is* the measurement.

A parameter counts as verified when it locks against two photos from different
viewpoints, so the set is built in pairs — two vault-overheads at different bays, two
axial views of the nave at different heights, two readings of each front.

## Notes

- **`ex-silhouette-2026`** is the building as it now stands: the Tower of Jesus Christ
  topped out, its four-armed cross placed in February 2026. It is the only frame in the
  set that shows the silhouette phase 4 is aiming at rather than an earlier state of it.

- **`plan-gaudi-1885`** is the drawing the 7.5 m module was measured off, scale bar
  included. Read it rather than trusting a number quoted about it.

- Several interior frames come from one 2009 survey by a single photographer
  (`in-nave-sw-up`, `in-vault-detail-funnels`, `in-column-clerestory-ene`,
  `in-nave-along-wide`, `ex-plaza-nativity`, `ex-apse-chapel-windows`). Their titles on
  Commons carry compass bearings, which is why they are here: a photograph that tells
  you which way it was facing constrains the camera before you start dragging sliders.

- **`in-light-shaft-on-floor`** has almost no edges in it and will not align to anything.
  It is a colour reference, not a geometry one — it is what the red light on the pavement
  actually looks like.

- Licences are CC BY, CC BY-SA or public domain. Attribution is only owed on
  redistribution, and these are not redistributed — but the record is kept here so that
  it exists if one ever leaves this directory.
